Create a high-quality square source artwork for a circular camera lens-cap
medallion inspired by the Soviet Helios-44-2 and the modern IronGlass-style
cinema rehousing of that vintage optic. This is a flat graphic specification,
not a product photograph and not a 3D lens rendering.

Exact rendered text, and no other text or digits anywhere: “58” must be the
largest and first visual read; “F2” must be the second-largest and second visual
read; secondary text may read only “HELIOS 44-2”, “REHOUSED CINEMA”, and “M42”.
Spell every character exactly. Do not add T*, T1.6, PL, Zeiss, Mamiya, Sigma,
Canon, Contax, Olympus, Batman, Dune, logos, trademarks, pseudo-text, or random
numerals.

Composition: a complete centered circle on a square canvas with a generous
clean edge. Make 58 and F2 dominant geometric typography, occupying most of
the face. Use bold condensed sans-serif letterforms, crisp straight edges,
and strong typographic contrast. Around them use broad connected black,
charcoal, medium-gray, and warm-ivory fields; continuous engraved/linocut or
screen-print lines; concentric optical swirls; one narrow radial searchlight
wedge; desert-like contour bands; and a clean segmented mechanical
registration grid. These motifs are an original, non-literal translation of
the qualified Helios-44-2 cinema-rehousing family association and Soviet lens
identity, not a film still or official film branding.

Palette strictly black, charcoal, gray, and warm ivory. Keep large fills flat
and production-friendly; allow antialiasing only at shape boundaries. No
gradients, halftone dots, stippling, paper grain, mottling, photographic
texture, glossy reflections, realistic shadows, lens barrel, glass, aperture
iris blades, camera, film strip, actors, spacecraft, film logos, Batman logo,
Dune logo, clutter, or rectangular crop. The circle must be fully visible and
suitable for deterministic same-canvas mask tracing. Typography and hierarchy
are more important than micro-detail.
